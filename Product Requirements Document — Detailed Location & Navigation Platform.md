# **Product Requirements Document — Detailed Location & Navigation Platform**

**Working product name:** TBD  
**Product type:** Location discovery, last-mile navigation & community-verified mapping  
**Primary market:** Nigeria initially, with potential expansion to other markets  
**Primary platform:** Mobile-first  
**Status:** Product concept / PRD v1

---

## **1\. Product Overview**

The product is a **more detailed location-identification and navigation platform** designed to solve a problem traditional map applications often struggle with:

> **Getting someone from the general area to the exact place they are trying to reach.**

Instead of stopping at a street address or map pin, the platform allows **property owners, tenants, residents, business owners and other trusted people** to create, describe, verify and maintain detailed location information.

A location could contain:

* Exact entrance/gate  
* Building identification  
* Floor/unit  
* Landmarks  
* Nearby recognizable objects  
* Turn-by-turn final approach instructions  
* Photos  
* Entrance photos  
* Security/gate instructions  
* Alternative entrances  
* Contact information  
* Traffic or road-condition alerts  
* Temporary access restrictions

The product should complement existing mapping services rather than initially trying to replace them.

### **Core proposition**

> **Maps get you close. We get you there.**

---

# **2\. Problem**

Traditional navigation systems are excellent at answering:

> “Where is this street?”

They can be less effective at answering:

> “Which of these three gates is the right one?”

> “Which building inside this compound am I looking for?”

> “What does the entrance actually look like?”

> “Where do I turn after reaching this landmark?”

> “The road is blocked today. How do I get there?”

> “I'm standing outside the estate. Which direction do I go?”

This is particularly relevant in environments where:

* Addresses aren't consistently structured.  
* Buildings have informal descriptions.  
* Streets contain multiple similar buildings.  
* Estates have several entrances.  
* Businesses operate inside larger buildings.  
* Landmarks are more recognizable than street names.  
* Roads or access routes change frequently.

---

# **3\. Product Vision**

Create a **human-verified layer of location information** that sits between a traditional map and the real-world destination.

The long-term vision is:

> **Every meaningful destination can have a digital identity that explains not just where it is, but how to recognize, enter and reach it.**

---

# **4\. Product Goals**

### **Primary goals**

1. Make difficult-to-find locations easier to reach.  
2. Allow people who know a location to provide useful directions.  
3. Make locations easier to recognize visually.  
4. Allow users to verify whether location information is accurate.  
5. Surface temporary road and traffic conditions.  
6. Build trust around location information through verification and community feedback.  
7. Create a detailed location profile for homes, businesses and other destinations.

### **Secondary goals**

* Reduce calls asking “Where exactly are you?”  
* Reduce failed deliveries and missed appointments.  
* Help visitors navigate large estates and compounds.  
* Improve discoverability of local businesses.  
* Build a continuously improving database of real-world places.

---

# **5\. What the Product Is NOT**

The product should **not initially try to become a complete replacement for Google Maps**.

It doesn't need to compete primarily on:

* Global road mapping  
* Satellite imagery  
* Public transport routing  
* Worldwide navigation  
* Comprehensive geographic databases

Instead, its advantage should be:

### **Location intelligence \+ human context \+ final-mile guidance.**

A user shouldn't simply receive:

# **13\. Location Verification**

Trust is extremely important.

A location can have different verification levels.

### **Suggested system**

**Unverified**

Created but not confirmed.

**Community Verified**

Multiple users have confirmed the information.

**Resident Verified**

A resident/tenant associated with the location has confirmed it.

**Owner Verified**

The property/business owner has claimed and verified it.

### **Important principle**

Verification should be visible but shouldn't make the interface intimidating.

Example:

> ✓ Owner verified  
> Updated 3 days ago

# **18\. Traffic Alert Experience**

Instead of simply displaying:

> "Traffic."

The product should explain:

> 🚧 **Road partially blocked**

> Heavy congestion reported around this junction.

> **Reported 12 minutes ago**

> Alternative route available.

Users can then confirm:

**Still happening**

or

**Cleared**

This prevents old reports from remaining indefinitely.

# **19\. Traffic Stop / Checkpoint Notifications**

Because you specifically mentioned traffic stops, this should be treated as a distinct category.

Examples:

> 🚔 Traffic stop reported ahead

> Police checkpoint reported on this road.

The product should present this **neutrally as a location/traffic condition**, rather than encouraging users to evade law enforcement.

For example:

**Reported checkpoint**

**Location:** XYZ Road  
**Reported:** 8 minutes ago  
**Status:** Active reports

Users can mark:

> Still present

or

> No longer present

# **32\. Safety & Privacy**

Because the platform deals with physical locations, privacy must be built into the product.

Users should have control over whether a location is:

* Public  
* Shared only through a private link  
* Visible to approved people

For residential locations, the product should avoid unnecessarily exposing sensitive information.

For example, a public profile might show:

> “Private residence — detailed directions available through shared link.”

rather than exposing someone's exact home details to everyone.

# **42\. Product Architecture at a Conceptual Level**

The product essentially has **four interconnected systems**:

### **1\. Places**

Where something physically exists.

### **2\. People**

Who knows, owns, lives at or manages that place.

### **3\. Directions**

How someone gets from the surrounding area to that place.

### **4\. Conditions**

What is happening around that place right now.

Together:

**PLACE \+ PEOPLE \+ DIRECTIONS \+ CONDITIONS**

creates something substantially richer than a conventional map pin.
